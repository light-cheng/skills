# Audience profiles

Pick the closest row. Adjust from what the user tells you about the person.

| Listener | Words and sentences | Good anchors | Length | What they want to know |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Young child (about 4–7) | Short words, one idea per sentence | Toys, food, family, animals, playground | 3–6 sentences | "What is it?" and "What does it do?" |
| Older child (about 8–12) | Everyday words, a few new ones defined | School, games, sports, building things | One short paragraph plus steps | How it works, step by step |
| Teenager | Normal speech, real terms with quick definitions | Phones, apps, social media, part-time jobs | A few paragraphs | Why it works and why it matters |
| Adult, no background | Plain language, no unexplained jargon | Home, money, cooking, travel, work errands | As long as needed, no longer | What it means for them in practice |
| Manager or executive | Outcome first, then mechanism | Cost, risk, time, customers, competitors | Short; lead with the decision | Impact, trade-offs, what to decide |
| Specialist from another field | Their field's terms are fine; yours need defining | Concepts from their own discipline | Medium | Where it matches and differs from what they know |
| Parent, partner or friend | Warm and conversational | Shared daily life | Short | Why the user cares about it |

## Grade levels

For "explain it to a 5th grader" and similar, use the child or teenager row
that matches the age, and keep the vocabulary to words that age reads
comfortably. A college student or graduate gets the "Adult, no background"
row, or the specialist row when their subject is close to the topic.

## When the request names two audiences

For example "so my boss can explain it to her team": write for the first
listener, and add one sentence they can pass on to the second.

## Checks before sending

- Could this listener retell the one idea in their own words?
- Is every technical word either defined or necessary?
- Did you say where the comparison breaks?
